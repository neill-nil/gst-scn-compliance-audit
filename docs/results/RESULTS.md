# NoticeCheck: final results, explained

**Status (15 Nov 2026): final.** All experiments are complete, including the synthetic stress set (section 16).
All numbers come from committed runs in `runs/`. The raw tables are regenerated by `python -m scripts.final_analysis`
into [`RESULTS_TABLES.md`](RESULTS_TABLES.md) and `runs/final/summary.json`; component metrics by
`python -m scripts.component_metrics` into `runs/final/components.json`. This file explains them.

## How to read the numbers
- **Test set:** 125 High Court judgments, never used while building the system. **Held-out court:** 12 Karnataka
  judgments. **Dev:** 49 cases used for tuning only.
- **What is scored:** each complaint the petitioner made *and the court decided*. Court found a defect + system says
  defect = **hit**; court rejected it + system says defect = **false alarm**; court found it + system says anything
  else (no defect, can't tell, contested) = **miss**. Complaints the court did not decide are not scored.
- **F1 (detection):** a defect call counts whatever section it cites. **Strict F1:** the cited section must also be
  right (a citation listing several sections counts if one is right). **Strict F1 is the measure our proposal named
  for RQ1.** NoticeCheck takes its sections from a fixed defect → section table, so its two scores are equal.
- **Unargued flags:** defects a system raised on points nobody argued. The court never ruled on them, so they are not
  scored as false alarms, but we report them; the fresh-case check (section 15) shows they are mostly wrong.
- **95% CI:** paired bootstrap, 5,000 resamples of the 125 cases. A difference whose interval excludes 0 is marked *.

## 1. What was built and compared

| System | Model | What it gets | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B0** | none (rules) | the facts record | floor |
| **B1** | Qwen3-8B, 4-bit, Ollama on Kaggle T4 | the facts record, one prompt | main baseline: same model as ours, no structure |
| **B2** | Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite (API) | the facts record + the text of the relevant sections pasted in | frontier reference |
| **NoticeCheck** | Qwen3-8B (same as B1), not fine-tuned | the facts record; plans tool calls, 8 rule checks + 5 model checks, law text by section lookup, verifier, hand-off | **our system** (3 runs) |
| **NoticeCheck + Gemini** | Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite | same pipeline, different model | does the structure help a big model? |
| **Ablations** | Qwen3-8B | NoticeCheck minus tools / retrieval / verifier / hand-off | what each part adds |
| **RQ2 extractors** | Qwen3-8B untrained; Qwen3-8B + our QLoRA adapter; Gemini drafts | the judgment text | fact extraction |
| **End to end** | fine-tuned (or untrained) extractor → NoticeCheck | the judgment text only | the full system, no human facts |

**Data.** 20,087 GST judgments screened from 24 High Courts (2024–2026) → 429 "reasoned" → Gemini drafts → reviewed by
the four of us in our review tool. Test complaints: 104 defects found, 38 rejected, 66 not decided. 35 cases were
reviewed twice independently: **Cohen's κ = 0.73** on 58 complaints (0.75 on the pairs done without AI help).
Courts in the gold set: Madras 52, Delhi 27, Allahabad 18, Karnataka 12, Bombay 10, others 18.

**Important framing.** Real show cause notices are not public. Every input is the proceeding (notice, reply, hearing,
order) **as described in a High Court judgment**, and the court's rulings are the answer key. The system never sees
the court's reasoning or decision.

## 2. Did the claim hold? (RQ1)

The approved proposal's claim: *beat the same 8B model given the record alone on defect-level F1 where type and cited
provision must match; falsified if the paired gain is under 10 points or its 95% CI includes 0, if our false-alarm
rate exceeds the baseline's, or if we do not beat the rule checklist on reasoning-dependent defects.*

| Test | Result | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Strict F1 ≥ 10 points above B1, CI above 0 | run 1 **+0.23** [+0.11, +0.35]; run 2 **+0.32** [+0.18, +0.44]; run 3 **+0.28** [+0.14, +0.40] | **Yes** |
| False-alarm rate not above B1's | B1 0.14; NoticeCheck 0.22, 0.22, 0.30 (8–11 false alarms vs 5, out of 37 rejected complaints) | **No** |
| Beat the rule checklist on reasoning-dependent defects | B0 finds none; NoticeCheck 0.34 / 0.52 / 0.44 | **Yes** |

**Two of three tests are met; the main one by a wide margin.** Two things must be said with it:
- The strict win comes mostly from **citing the right section**: B1 cites a wrong one on 14 of its 27 hits (e.g.
  s.75(4) or s.73(9) for an ignored reply, where the rule is s.75(6)); NoticeCheck never does. On **detection alone**
  the gain over B1 is +0.03 / +0.11 / +0.07 and **not significant**.
- The extra false alarms are mostly on **service** (portal-upload cases, section 8).

*On the synthetic stress set (section 16), with every fact stated, all three tests are met: strict gain +0.67, false alarms on clean records 7 vs B1's 15, and model-judged defects 0.81 vs none for B0; the detection gain over B1 (+0.17) is also significant there.*

*An earlier draft of the claim was about GHMC building notices and 150 synthetic notices with clean controls. The
approved proposal replaced it with GST proceedings and real court labels. Its "false alarms on valid notices" idea is
what the synthetic stress set (section 16) tests.*

## 5. Run-to-run variation
The three NoticeCheck runs differ significantly (run 2 − run 1 = +0.08 *) although temperature is 0: Ollama batches
parallel requests on the GPU, so outputs are not exactly repeatable. The runs gave the **same verdict on 89%** of
case × defect pairs. A majority vote of the three scores 0.45, inside the range. **This is why we report three runs
and their range, and why small differences (under ~0.08) cannot be read as real.**

## 6. Notice-only view
Given only what was known on the day of the notice: NoticeCheck 0.29, B2 0.30, B0 0.27 (F1). Most procedural defects
happen after the notice (hearing, reply, order), so with the notice alone most checks are correctly "can't tell".
NoticeCheck raised 7 unargued flags in this view.

## 8. Error analysis: why NoticeCheck misses and where it false-alarms

**Misses (run 2, 104 court-found defects):** NoticeCheck said *defect* on 38, **can't tell on 49**, contested on 10,
no defect on 7.
- **Hearing is the big one: 29 of the 49 "can't tell".** Judgments state the conclusion ("the order was passed
  without affording an opportunity of hearing", "admittedly ex parte") but rarely the facts the check needs (whether
  a hearing date was given, when). Our extraction rules keep the court's opinion out of the facts (otherwise the input
  would leak the answer), so the check correctly answers "can't tell".
- **All 10 "one notice for several years" misses are by design:** High Courts disagree (Delhi vs Bombay lines), so
  NoticeCheck answers *contested* and asks a lawyer to decide. The scorer counts that as a miss. NoticeCheck flags
  81% of the 26 contested points in the test set.
- **No defect where the court found one (7):** e.g. it trusted the petitioner's account that a hearing was offered
  when the court found none.
- We tested a rule "order + no hearing facts → defect" on dev: 33 flags, only 4 right. Rejected.

**False alarms on argued complaints:** run 1: service 4, reply 2, other 1, reasons 1; run 2: service 4, other 2,
reply 1, reasons 1; run 3: service 4, reply 4, other 1, limitation 1, reasons 1 (B1: service 3; B2: service 4). The
**service** false alarms are portal-upload cases: NoticeCheck treats "uploaded only to the Additional Notices tab" as
defective service (several High Courts do), and in these cases the court disagreed.

## 9. Ablations (test, one run each)

| Version | F1 | Precision | Recall | False alarms | Unargued flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full NoticeCheck (runs 1–3) | 0.42–0.51 | 0.76–0.83 | 0.29–0.37 | 8–11 | 29–33 |
| No tools (model judges dates itself) | 0.48 | 0.83 | 0.34 | 7 | **48** |
| No retrieval (no law text to the model) | 0.49 | 0.84 | 0.35 | 7 | 30 |
| No verifier (findings not checked against the record) | 0.45 | 0.77 | 0.32 | **10** | 30 |
| No hand-off (never escalates) | 0.46 | 0.82 | 0.32 | 7 | 32 |

**Every ablation lands inside the full system's run-to-run range**, so on 125 cases we cannot say which component
matters most for F1. Two signals: **without tools, unargued flags rise from ~30 to 48**; **without the verifier,
false alarms are highest**. "No retrieval" changes little because the model already knows what the main sections say
and the citation itself comes from the section table (section 10). We report this as a finding about how hard it is
to measure components on a small real test set, not as evidence that the parts are useless.

## 10. The five capabilities: what each contributed (component metrics)

| Capability (proposal weight) | Metric promised | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RAG** (core) | Recall@k of gold provisions; citation faithfulness | Law text fetched by exact section (`statute_lookup`, ~310 valid calls per run); **0 wrong sections** vs 9–14 for the baselines; every defect quote verified to exist in the record; abstains ("can't tell") without evidence. Case-law search backs contested points (81% flagged) | **Partly**: strong grounding and citation, weak search, which the design avoids relying on |
| **Reasoning** (core) | Accuracy of each intermediate check | 13 separate checks scored individually (section 7); rule vs reasoning split; miss analysis by step (section 8); verifier analysis; contested law flagged, not decided | **Delivered and analysed**; gains clear on strict F1, modest on detection |
| **Tool calling** (core) | Tool-call accuracy; unsafe-action rate | ~630 model tool calls per run, **95–97% valid arguments** (Gemini 100%); time-limit calculator used in **90–98%** of cases where the dates allow it; recovered from an argument error in only 15 of 50 cases; **0 unsafe actions**. | **Fine-tuning** (moderate) | Field F1 vs large prompted model | 0.52 → **0.80** (0.88 repaired) vs Gemini drafts 0.95 (inflated); section 13 | **Strong** |

**Verifier:** it changed 4–7 defect calls per run to "can't tell" for lack of a real quote; across the three runs only
**1** of these was a defect the court had found (the rest: points nobody argued or the court did not decide).

**Cost per case (NoticeCheck, Qwen on one T4):** 2.2 model calls, ~4,500 tokens in, ~3,500 out, ~3 minutes, plus ~5
cheap tool calls. B1: 1 call, ~1,100 in / ~2,100 out, ~2 minutes. B2 and NoticeCheck + Gemini: ~5–10 seconds per case
on the free API.

## 11. NoticeCheck with Gemini: structure vs model
Putting Gemini inside our pipeline gives **0.51 F1, the same as B2 (0.50)**: the pipeline does not make Gemini detect
more. But with the right section required it beats B2 by **+0.13 *** (B2 cites a wrong section on 9 hits), and it has
the same restraint as B2 (14 unargued flags). With either model the pipeline lands near 0.50, which supports the view
that **the ceiling comes from what the records contain, not from the model.**

## 12. Held-out court (Karnataka, 12 cases, 6 court-found defects)
Every system working from checked facts finds **0 of 6** (B0, B1, B2, all NoticeCheck runs, NoticeCheck + Gemini).
Karnataka judgments are short: records have 0–1 notices and no hearing facts, so every system says "can't tell". The
end-to-end system (reading the raw judgment) found 1 of 6. With 6 defects this is a limitation, not a measurement.

## 13. RQ2: fine-tuning the fact extractor

**Training:** QLoRA on Qwen3-8B (Unsloth, 4-bit, rank 16, 2 epochs, loss on the answer only), **196 training
examples** (Gemini drafts of other judgments; no test, dev or held-out case), 50 steps in **74 minutes on one free
T4**; train loss 0.057, validation loss 0.037. Same prompt for every extractor (instructions + output format + the
judgment cut at 12,000 characters).

| Extractor (125 test judgments) | Field F1 | Precision | Recall | Events F1 | Doc contents F1 | Schema-valid | Quotes found in judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Untrained Qwen3-8B | 0.52 | 0.38 | 0.82 | 0.42 | 0.40 | 31/125 | 88% |
| **Fine-tuned Qwen3-8B** | **0.80** | **0.90** | 0.72 | 0.71 | 0.56 | 103/125 | 96% |
| Fine-tuned, cut-off outputs repaired | 0.88 | 0.91 | 0.84 | 0.72 | 0.55 | – | 96% |
| Gemini drafts (inflated) | 0.95 | 0.96 | 0.94 | 0.96 | 0.97 | 125/125 | 97% |

- **Fine-tuning works: +0.28 field F1** (CI [+0.22, +0.34] *); held-out Karnataka 0.50 → 0.89.
- **The untrained model invents values** (precision 0.38) and rarely follows the format (31/125 schema-valid).
- **Weak spots:** 22 fine-tuned outputs hit the 2,048-token limit, mostly by repeating the same quote; "repaired"
  keeps the valid beginning and closes the brackets (same rule for every system). Hearing fields stay hard
  (hearing date given 0.17, hearing date 0.22), as for the checker.
- **The Gemini number is inflated:** our reviewers corrected those very drafts to build the answer key.
- **RQ2 answer:** the tuned 8B model **does not match** the large model, but it closes most of the gap and makes the
  small-model pipeline usable end to end.

## 14. End to end: judgment in, defect report out

| NoticeCheck reading facts from | F1 = strict F1 | Precision | Recall | Unargued flags | Defect calls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Our checked facts (run 2) | 0.51 | 0.83 | 0.37 | 29 | 84 |
| **Fine-tuned extractor (the full system)** | **0.38** | 0.79 | 0.25 | 31 | 70 |
| Untrained extractor | 0.56 | 0.85 | 0.42 | **97** | **158** |

- **The full small-model system works end to end** with the same precision and restraint; extraction errors cost
  about 0.09 F1, mostly recall. It still **beats B1 on strict F1 (+0.19 *)** even though B1 was given checked facts.
- **The untrained extractor's higher F1 is built on invented facts:** it filled in "hearing date given" in 77 of 125
  cases where the judgment says nothing, the service method in 80, the signature in 57. NoticeCheck then flags
  defects almost everywhere (158 calls, 97 unargued), and 39% of its evidence quotes are not in the judgment (24% for
  the fine-tuned extractor, 18% with checked facts; partly because judgments are cut at 12,000 characters). Because
  courts upheld most argued complaints, blanket flagging often lands. The same happens with B0 (0.54 on untrained
  facts, 39 unargued flags). **Defect F1 alone can reward a hallucinating extractor;** field accuracy, unargued flags
  and quote grounding expose it.

Picked on 14 Nov after the system was frozen, from cases nobody had used (every unused "reasoned" case was a
duplicate, so these came from the screen's other buckets after a Gemini filter). The full system ran end to end
(Kaggle notebook H); the four of us checked its 81 calls against the judgments in a purpose-built check page.

| NoticeCheck said | Calls | Correct | Wrong | Can't verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| defect | 19 | 8 | 9 | 2 |
| contested | 16 | 10 | 4 | 2 |
| no defect | 46 | 33 | 2 | 11 |

- **Every correct defect call was a defect the court found (8 of 8). All 6 defect calls on points nobody argued were
  judged wrong.** This is direct evidence that unargued flags are mostly false alarms, which makes NoticeCheck's lower
  count (~30 vs B1's 88) matter.
- "No defect" was right in 94% of verifiable calls; "contested" fit the case in 71%.
- About **14 court-found defects were missed** in 10 of 20 cases, mostly "no hearing" in the Madras portal-service

- Caveats: reviewers saw the system's answer (risk of agreeing with it); two declared AI assistance; single review.

## 16. Synthetic stress set
**Why:** the proposal promised a synthetic stress set, and it is the only way to measure **false alarms on valid
proceedings** (clean controls) and **detection when every fact is stated**. Real judgments omit many facts and contain
no case known to be fully valid.

**Design** (`scripts/make_synthetic.py`, `data/synthetic/`): 100 records, seed 15. 8 each for 7 defect types (no
hearing; unsigned notice or order; summary-only notice; notice not served or only on the Additional Notices tab; time
limit missed (order or notice late, FY 2021-22 and 2022-23, no extension issues); reply ignored; order without
reasons) = 56 defective, 44 clean. Every record states all facts the 7 checks need; the planted type is labelled
defect_found and the other 6 defect_rejected, giving **56 planted defects and 644 clean labelled checks**. NoticeCheck
(Qwen) and B1 ran on Kaggle (notebook S, same settings as the test runs); B0, B2 and NoticeCheck + Gemini on a laptop.

| System | F1 | Precision | Recall | Strict F1 | Wrong-section hits | False alarms on 644 clean checks | Rule-type F1 | Model-judged F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 rules | 0.40 | 1.00 | 0.25 | n/a | – | 0 (0.0%) | 0.61 | – |
| B1 Qwen3-8B, record only | 0.74 | 0.74 | 0.75 | 0.25 | 28 | 15 (2.3%) | 0.68 | 0.83 |
| B2 Gemini + statute | 0.91 | 1.00 | 0.84 | 0.58 | 17 | 0 (0.0%) | 0.84 | 1.00 |
| **NoticeCheck (Qwen3-8B)** | **0.91** | 0.88 | **0.95** | **0.91** | **0** | 7 (1.1%) | **1.00** | 0.81 |
| NoticeCheck + Gemini | 0.99 | 0.98 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0 | 1 (0.2%) | 1.00 | 0.98 |

| Comparison | Δ F1 [95% CI] | Δ strict F1 [95% CI] | Δ false alarms [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|
| NoticeCheck − B1 (same model) | **+0.17 [+0.08, +0.27] *** | **+0.67 [+0.55, +0.77] *** | −8 [−18, +1] |
| NoticeCheck − B2 | +0.00 [−0.08, +0.09] | **+0.33 [+0.20, +0.47] *** | +7 [+2, +13] * |
| NoticeCheck + Gemini − B2 (same model) | **+0.08 [+0.02, +0.14] *** | **+0.41 [+0.28, +0.54] *** | +1 [0, +3] |
| NoticeCheck + Gemini − NoticeCheck (Qwen) | **+0.08 [+0.03, +0.13] *** | **+0.08 *** | **−6 [−12, −1] *** |

**What the synthetic set shows.**
- **With the facts stated, NoticeCheck clearly beats the same model used directly, even on detection:** +0.17 F1
  (significant), which the real test set could not show because its records lack the facts. With the right section
  required the gap is +0.67 (B1 cites a wrong section on 28 of its 42 hits, mostly "s.73" or "s.73(9)").
- **Time limits are where tools matter most.** B1 caught only 2 of 8 late orders **and raised 11 false time-limit
  alarms on clean records**; B2 (statute text, no calculator) missed all 8 and said "no defect" on 7. NoticeCheck, which
  calls the limitation calculator, caught all 8 with no false alarm, with either model.
- **False alarms on valid proceedings:** NoticeCheck (Qwen) 7 of 644 (1.1%) vs B1 15 (2.3%), half as many, though the
  interval just includes zero; B2 0; NoticeCheck + Gemini 1. Six of NoticeCheck's 7 are "order without reasons" on
  records whose planted defect was the ignored reply (the order quotes figures but never mentions the reply), so the
  two model-judged types overlap.
- **Rule-based types are perfect (1.00) for NoticeCheck** with both models. This is partly by construction: the rules
  are code over facts that are all present, so it mainly confirms the rules and the calculator work as intended.
- **Model-judged types (reply ignored, no reasons) depend on the model:** Qwen 0.81 (B1 0.83), Gemini 0.98–1.00. The
  structure does not make the small model better at these judgement calls; a stronger model does.
- **Against B2:** equal on detection (0.91 each), NoticeCheck ahead on citations (+0.33) but with more false alarms
  (7 vs 0). Putting Gemini inside the pipeline beats B2 on every measure except one extra false alarm.

**RQ1's three tests on the synthetic set:** strict F1 gain over B1 +0.67 with the interval above zero (**met**);
false alarms on clean records 7 vs B1's 15 (**met** in direction, not significant); beat B0 on model-judged defects
0.81 vs B0's none (**met**). On real judgments the false-alarm test failed (section 2); on clean synthetic records,
NoticeCheck raises fewer false alarms than B1.

## 17. Other notes
- **Dev vs test:** NoticeCheck scored 0.68 on the 49 dev cases and 0.47 on test. Dev was easier (partly drafts) and
  we tuned on it; the test number is the honest one.
- **Earlier numbers:** the mid report used 52 test cases (B2 0.56 then, 0.50 on the full 125). Tables shared during
  the project before 15 Nov used detection F1 only; strict F1 was added in the final analysis, with the scorer fixed
  to accept multi-section citations.
- **Freeze:** NoticeCheck was frozen on 13 Nov after one bug fix found on dev (the verifier rejected valid quotes that
  carried the prompt's `record: "..."` wrapper). Nothing was changed after seeing test results.

## 18. Limitations
1. **No real notices:** inputs are proceedings as described in judgments, not notice documents.
2. **Judgments omit facts:** conclusions without dates cap recall for every system; "can't tell" is often correct.
3. **Gemini drafted the answer key** before human correction, which may favour B2 and inflates the Gemini extraction score.
4. **Small test set:** 125 cases, 104 found defects; differences under ~0.08–0.10 F1 are not provable, and the
   ablations cannot separate components.
5. **Run-to-run variation** in the local model, even at temperature 0.
6. **Unargued flags cannot be scored** automatically; the fresh check covers only 20 cases.
7. **Uneven courts:** Madras and Delhi dominate; the held-out court has only 6 defects.
8. **Fresh-check labels** were made by reviewers who saw the system's answer.
9. **Synthetic records are template-built:** the rule-based checks are perfect partly by construction, and the records are cleaner and more uniform than real proceedings.
