Source Judgment Paragraphs

Synchronized Source Record
¶ 1
The present writ petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India has been instituted challenging the impugned adjudication order passed by Respondent No. 2 under Section 73 of the CGST Act, 2017. The records reveal that the SCN issued on 12.05.2024 for tax period FY 2018-19 proposed an aggregate tax demand of ₹42,18,910 on the alleged ground of mismatched input tax credit between Form GSTR-3B and GSTR-2A.
¶ 2
Learned Counsel for the petitioner submits that the petitioner was oblivious to the issuance of the notice because the notice was uploaded only to the portal without physical or email communication under the additional notices tab, and the petitioner came to know of the proceedings only upon receiving an attachment memo from their commercial bank.
¶ 4
A perusal of the original record produced by the revenue demonstrates that on 28.06.2024, the petitioner submitted an interim representation which was unconsidered in the final adjudication. The Proper Officer proceeded to finalize the demand on 18.10.2024 without referring to any ground or reconciliations enclosed with the said representation.
¶ 6
Crucially, statutory mandate under Section 75(4) demands that an opportunity of hearing shall be granted where a request is received or where any adverse decision is contemplated. In the case at hand, Column 4 of Form DRC-07 remained blank and no opportunity of personal hearing was granted to the petitioner prior to creating the liability.
¶ 8
Consequently, this Court finds that the impugned order violates the statutory safeguards enacted under Section 75(4) and Section 75(6) of the Act. The order dated 18.10.2024 is hereby set aside with direction to the Proper Officer to issue a fresh notice for personal hearing.

Clinical Tax Law Legal-Tech Experimentation

Research Overview & Research Questions

NoticeCheck was evaluated on High Court judgments reciting GST proceedings under Sections 73 & 74, not on original show cause notices or raw departmental files. Rather than predicting case outcomes or rendering legal advice, NoticeCheck evaluates whether procedural statutory requirements (Personal Hearing under S.75(4), Reasoned Orders under S.75(6), and Limitation deadlines under S.73/74) were respected as described in the judicial record.

RQ1: Checker Pipeline Comparison

Comparing Plain Qwen vs. NoticeCheck's rule-guided pipeline on an identical verified fact record. Observation: The structured pipeline was generally more restrained about raising unargued issues; it did not conclusively meet the planned F1 improvement threshold.

RQ2: Fact Extraction Precision

Comparing base Qwen vs. fine-tuned Qwen on the raw appellate text. Fine-tuned extraction ties values to paragraph citations and preserves unstated entries as explicit null ("Date not stated in record").

schoolTeaching Methodology & Hand-off Notice

Designed for academic demonstration and clinical training on procedural due process under Indian tax statutes. Outputs strictly procedural findings and a human review recommendation without determining final litigation decisions.
